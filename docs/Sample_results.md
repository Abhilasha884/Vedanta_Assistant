## Test Case 2: Managing Anger

**User Question:**

How can I control my anger?

**Generated Response:**

I understand how frustrating it can be when anger starts to build up inside you. The Bhagavad Gita teaches that anger, like desire, can obscure our understanding and lead us to act impulsively. When we feel anger, it’s often because our senses are not well controlled, which makes it hard to see things clearly.

So, when you notice anger rising, try to pause for a moment. Ask yourself: *“What is this anger trying to tell me?”* Then, gently bring your attention to your breath or a calming object to help control your senses. This small act of awareness and discipline can prevent impulsive actions and help you respond with more thoughtfulness.

Remember, it’s not about eliminating anger entirely, but about understanding it so you don’t let it drive your decisions. You’re doing great by seeking this guidance.

## Test Case 3: Worrying About the Future

**User Question:**

I constantly worry about my future and cannot focus on the present.

**Generated Response:**

It's completely understandable to feel worried about the future and find it hard to stay present. The Bhagavad Gita teaches that when we're overwhelmed by emotions like worry, it can cloud our ability to see clearly and focus on the moment we're in. This teaching also reminds us that the eternal Self is active *right now*, while the future—like the body or temporary circumstances—is subject to change and impermanence.

When worry starts to build, try pausing for just a few seconds and asking: *“What is this worry trying to protect me from?”* Then gently bring your attention to your breath, a sound, or the feeling of your feet on the ground. This small shift helps anchor you in the present without judgment.

Remember, it's okay to feel worried—this isn't a sign of weakness but a natural part of being human. The key is to notice the worry without getting caught in it, and to trust that the present moment holds your true peace and clarity.

## Test Case 4: Disappointment Despite Hard Work

**User Question:**

I work hard but feel disappointed when I do not get the expected results.

**Generated Response:**

It's really common to feel disappointed when you work hard but don't get the results you expected. The Bhagavad Gita teaches that when we focus on doing our duty with care, without getting attached to the results, we can avoid this kind of disappointment.

Next time you feel this disappointment, try pausing for a moment and asking: *'What is this disappointment trying to tell me?'* Then, gently bring your attention to the present effort without expecting a specific outcome. This small shift helps you stay connected to the process rather than the result.

Remember, it's natural to feel this way, but by shifting your focus from the outcome to the effort, you can find peace in the work itself.
